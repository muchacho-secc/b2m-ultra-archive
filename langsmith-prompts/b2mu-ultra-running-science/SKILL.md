## RACE DAY LOGISTICS — B2M SPECIFIC

# 

```
Bus departure: Manly at 3:45am → Bondi
Arrive Bondi: 1 hour before start time minimum
Drop bag: Submit at Bondi Pavilion (Fri 12pm-6pm OR Sat 4am-6:15am)
           Transported to FINISH LINE at Manly — NOT a mid-race drop
Race start: Waves from 5am, Bondi Pavilion
Finish: Manly Beach
Spectators: Ferry from Circular Quay recommended

Aid Stations (official):
  ~20km — Lyne Park, Rose Bay (Leg 1 checkpoint)
  ~40km — Milson Park, North Sydney (Leg 2 checkpoint — drop head torch here)
  ~60km — Clontarf Beach Reserve (Leg 3 checkpoint)
  ~70km — East Esplanade, Manly (CRITICAL — last fuel before North Head)

Mandatory gear (strictly enforced — 10 min penalty per missing item):
  Head torch (carry until Aid Station 2 at ~40km, then drop)
  Water capacity 800ml minimum
  Mobile phone
  Compression bandage
  Thermal blanket
  Rain jacket (weather dependent)
```

**Critical drop bag clarification:** The drop bag goes to the FINISH LINE — not to a mid-race checkpoint. Pack finish line recovery items: dry clothes, food, sandals. Do NOT rely on drop bag for mid-race nutrition. Carry everything needed. Aid stations provide food and water — plan around those, not a drop bag pickup.

## PACING STRATEGY — ULTRA SPECIFIC

# 

### The Golden Rule

# 

Start 15-20% slower than you think you should. Every time. The B2M course is described as "constantly undulating — no flat sections, no single steady rhythm." Pace by EFFORT, not by pace.

### Effort-Based Pacing by Leg

# 

**Leg 1 (19.94km, Bondi → Rose Bay):**

- Target effort: RPE 5-6 — feels embarrassingly easy
- This is where the race is most often LOST, not won
- Scenery creates excitement-pacing — resist it
- Faster road sections tempt early speed — resist it
- Rule: if it feels easy, you're doing it right

**Leg 2 (20.81km, Rose Bay → Milson Park):**

- Target effort: RPE 6-7 — controlled and sustainable
- Highest elevation gain despite looking urban and flat
- Opera House and Harbour Bridge sections lift mood DO NOT let that inflate effort
- Fuelling becomes critical here — never skip an aid station

**Leg 3 (23.24km, Milson Park → Clontarf):**

- Target effort: RPE 7 — working but controlled
- Fatigue compounds here — expect it, don't panic
- Walk all technical stair sections — running them costs more than it saves
- Fuelling discipline wins or loses the race on this leg
- Eat at EVERY aid station whether hungry or not

**Leg 4 (17.81km, Clontarf → Manly via North Head):**

- Target effort: RPE 8-9 — everything left
- 60km in legs when North Head stairs begin
- Uneven stair heights break rhythm — shorten stride, use hands on rail
- "Deliberate psychological cruelty" — train this section specifically
- Most runners blow up here — your training for this is what separates you

### Walk/Run Strategy

# 

Walking is not failure — it is strategy.

Walk these — every time, no exception:

- Any climb steeper than 8-10% grade
- All stair sections
- When HR exceeds 85% max
- When eating or drinking (prevents GI issues)
- The first 2km (warm-up discipline)

Run these:

- All flat and runnable sections
- Gentle descents (controlled, not bombing)
- Sections where you feel genuinely good — bank time here

**The math:** Walking 1km takes \~10-12 minutes at ultra pace. Running that same km takes \~7-8 minutes. The difference is 3-4 minutes. Walking a hard climb properly costs 3 minutes and saves your legs for 30km. Running it costs 3 minutes of leg damage you can never recover. Walk early — run late.

## FUELLING SCIENCE

# 

### Carbohydrate Targets

# 

For a 9-hour effort: aim for 60-90g carbohydrates per hour. Formula: your body weight in kg ≈ carbohydrates per hour you need. At \~80kg: target 80g/hr.

Sources:

- Energy gels: typically 20-25g carbs each
- At 80g/hr: 3-4 gels per hour OR combination of gels + real food
- Dual-source carbs (glucose + fructose) allow higher absorption rates
- From hour 5+: real food increasingly important (gut tolerates solid food better)

### Hydration Targets

# 

- Drink to thirst — do not over-drink
- Target: 400-800ml fluid per hour (adjust for heat)
- Sodium: minimum 300-600mg per hour to prevent hyponatremia
- Sports drinks at aid stations — take them, don't skip for water only
- Warning: drinking too much plain water without sodium = hyponatremia risk Symptoms: nausea, confusion, headache. Cause: diluted sodium levels. Prevention: electrolytes with every drink, salty food at aid stations

### Fuelling Timeline for B2M

# 

```
Pre-race (3am): Breakfast — easily digestible carbs
                Toast + jam or oatmeal or banana
                Low fat, low protein, low fibre
                Sip electrolyte drink, not plain water

Bus to Bondi:   Small snack if >2hrs since breakfast
                Avoid anything heavy or new

KM 0-10:       Don't eat yet — stomach needs to settle
               Sip regularly at aid stations

KM 10 onwards: Begin fuelling — every 30-40 minutes
               Never wait until hungry. By the time you feel it, it's too late.

KM 20+:        Aid station every time — gel OR real food OR both
               Keep sodium coming in

KM 40+:        Real food priority — gut prefers solids now
               Fruit, sandwiches, rice balls if available
               Keep gels as backup, not primary

KM 60+:        Caffeine gel or cola if available
               Mental boost when legs are gone
               Keep eating even if nausea — stop eating = bonk = DNF risk

KM 70-80:      Whatever it takes — dig into reserves
               This is where fuelling discipline from km 10-60 pays off
```

### B2M Specific Fuelling Notes

# 

- Aid stations: 4 stations + 3 water points across the course
- Carry enough gels between stations — don't assume spacing
- Drop bag goes to FINISH LINE only — cannot use mid-race
- Practice this fuelling strategy on every long run 16km+ in training
- Gut training takes weeks — cannot improvise on race day

## MENTAL STRATEGIES — ULTRA SPECIFIC

# 

These are evidence-based strategies from ultra running coaches and athletes. The coach should reference these during training and race preparation.

**1. Break it into legs, not kilometres** B2M has four natural legs with checkpoints. Don't run 80km. Run to Rose Bay. Then to Milson Park. Then to Clontarf. Then to Manly. Four separate races. Focus only on the next checkpoint.

**2. Expect low patches — they always pass** Every ultra runner hits low patches. They are temporary. When one hits: check eating, check drinking, check pacing. Almost every low patch has a physical cause. Fix the cause. Do not make decisions about quitting during a low patch.

**3. Have a mantra** Simple and personal. Examples: "Strong and steady", "Just keep moving", "One more km", "I've trained for this." Use it at the top of climbs. Use it when the voice says stop.

**4. Turn pain into data** Instead of "this hurts" → "my quads are working hard — that's normal at km 65." Observing rather than reacting keeps you rational when your brain wants to quit.

**5. Visualise North Head before race day** The biggest psychological shock in B2M is seeing Manly Wharf at km 73 — 200m from the finish line — then being routed UP and around North Head. Athletes who don't know this happens often break mentally. Athletes who have trained on North Head with tired legs don't. This is why the Leg 4 recce is mandatory.

**6. Smile — genuinely or fake it** Smiling reduces perceived effort. Use it at the top of climbs. Use it at aid stations. It works even when you don't mean it.

**7. Focus on your why** Months of early mornings, missed social events, long runs in the heat. That investment is the reason to keep moving when legs say stop.

**8. Run your own race** Ignore what other runners are doing. The person who passes you at km 10 will likely walk past you at km 65. Stay disciplined to your plan.

**9. Be adaptable** Things will go wrong. A blister, a GI issue, a navigational wrong turn. Problem-solve and keep moving. Forward is the only direction.

## STAIR TECHNIQUE

# 

The B2M course has significant stair sections, primarily in Legs 3 and 4. Your biggest physical gap is stair tolerance — address this specifically.

**Going up stairs:**

- Shorten stride significantly — no bounding
- Use hands on rail — this is not weakness, it is efficiency
- Lean forward from hips, not waist
- Drive with glutes, not quads — saves quads for the flats
- Walk all stair climbs — running up stairs late in an ultra is almost never faster overall

**Going down stairs:**

- Shorten stride — quick, light steps
- Land midfoot, not heel
- Lean slightly forward — leaning back creates brake forces and hammers quads
- Eyes 3-4 steps ahead, not at feet
- Hold rail on technical or uneven stairs (Clontarf section)
- The descent from North Head to Manly: "not particularly technical but with 70+km in the legs it felt more challenging" — Fred Francis

**Training prescription for stairs:**

- Minimum 1 stair session per week from BUILD phase
- Coogee Stairmaster, Bronte stairs, Bondi Icebergs stairs
- Progress from: climb only → climb + descent → repeat sets
- Target by PEAK phase: 10+ sets of significant stair climbs in a session

## TRAINING PRINCIPLES — ULTRA SPECIFIC

# 

### 80/20 Rule (confirmed by B2M coaches)

# 

80% of all training volume at easy aerobic effort (RPE 3-4, conversational) 20% at hard effort (hill repeats, stair sets, tempo) Consistent easy running builds the aerobic base that carries you through Leg 4.

### Hill and Stair Training is Non-Negotiable

# 

Multiple experienced B2M runners confirm: "Hill sessions are what really made the difference." Generic flat running does not prepare you for 1,684m of cumulative elevation on tired legs.

### Course Familiarity Matters Enormously

# 

"I ran all parts of the course a number of times to get used to the changing terrain and to understanding my pacing." — Craig Johnson Run each leg before race day. Not to preview scenery — to know pacing.

### Long Run Progression

# 

Experienced B2M coaches recommend:

- Get in a few 40km+ runs and 2+ 50km+ runs in focused 8-12 week block
- Long runs teach the body to run on depleted glycogen
- Long runs teach the gut to accept fuelling while moving
- Long runs teach the mind that discomfort is survivable

### Strength Training Complements Running

# 

Multiple B2M athletes mention skipping strength sessions as a mistake. Target: 2x per week strength, focused on:

- Single-leg exercises (split squats, step-ups) — stair specific
- Hip stability (glute work) — injury prevention on technical terrain
- Core stability — form maintenance in Leg 4

## OFFICIAL COURSE ELEVATION PROFILE — KEY INSIGHTS

# 

Source: Official B2M race website (confirmed 1,648m total gain) Max elevation: \~190m above sea level Course character: Never high — never flat. Relentlessly rolling.

**What the profile reveals:**

Km 0-9 (Leg 1 start): Most aggressive early climbing — 3 significant peaks in first 9km Cliff top section out of Bondi Most dangerous section for going out too hard COACHING RULE: If it feels easy, you're doing it right

Km 9-52 (Legs 1-2 and Leg 3 start): Consistent rolling terrain 0-40m height Deceptively runnable — this is where pacing discipline pays off Most runners make their fatal mistake here by running too fast

Km 52-65 (Leg 3 mid): Significant spike — sharp climb around km 61-65 Cremorne/Bradleys Head section — more climbing than it looks Fatigue is compounding here — walk all climbs

Km 65-79 (Leg 4 — North Head): THREE distinct sharp spikes in final 15km After 65km of accumulated fatigue, the sharpest repeated climbs begin This is why North Head breaks unprepared runners This is why training Leg 4 on tired legs is mandatory

**The deception:** 1,648m total on a course that never exceeds 190m altitude means the same terrain is climbed and descended repeatedly. Not one big mountain. 80km of relentless small hills that accumulate. Train the accumulation — not the individual climbs.

## SOTA MAEHERA — B2M ULTRA COACHING FRAMEWORK

# 

Source: Sota Maehera (ultra coach, YouTube channel) This is the highest-quality B2M specific coaching framework available. The coach should reference these principles directly.

### THE HIGHEST ROI TRAINING INPUTS FOR B2M

# 

**A. Long Runs — single biggest predictor of finishing well**

- Mid-pack runners: build to 3-4 hours
- Stronger runners: build to 4-5 hours
- Pace: easy, conversational, lots of hiking
- This athlete targets sub-9 hours → target 4-5 hour long runs in PEAK phase

**B. Back-to-Back Long Days — teaches legs to run tired** Essential for B2M's late climbs. This is non-negotiable in PEAK phase.

```
Example PEAK back-to-back:
  Saturday: 3-4 hours (Leg 3 recce — 23km technical trail)
  Sunday:   2-3 hours (Leg 4 recce — 18km North Head)
Keep BOTH easy. Goal is durability, not speed.
```

The back-to-back specifically trains what Leg 4 demands: running on tired legs with accumulated stair fatigue.

**C. Time on Feet — underrated, essential** B2M is 8-14 hours of continuous movement. The body must tolerate this, not just be fit enough.

"Standing = Training" — Sota's most underrated principle:

- Stand during work calls
- Walk instead of sit during breaks
- Add 30-60 min easy walking daily
- This builds foot strength, tendon resilience, lower-leg durability
- Free training that adds zero injury risk

**D. Nutrition Practice — gut training takes weeks**

```
Target: 60-90g carbs/hr
Mix: sweet + savoury (palate fatigue is real at hour 7+)
Hydration: electrolytes every time, not plain water
Practice on every long run 16km+
You cannot improvise gut training on race day
```

**E. Course-Specificity — train what the course demands**

- Stairs
- Short, sharp climbs
- Technical sandstone
- Heat exposure (train in warm conditions)
- Coastal wind
- Urban path transitions The training must look like the race.

---

### STRENGTH TRAINING — ESSENTIAL, NOT OPTIONAL

# 

Sota is clear: strength training matters for B2M specifically.

**Why it's essential:**

- Protects knees on endless stairs
- Helps with late-race form collapse (km 60-80)
- Improves climbing efficiency
- Reduces injury risk during high volume blocks

**What to include (2-3x per week):**

Lower body: Step-ups (weighted) ← stair-specific Split squats / Bulgarian squats Deadlifts (Romanian or trap bar) Calf raises (heavy + high-rep)

Core: Planks, side planks Anti-rotation (Pallof press)

Posterior chain: Hip thrusts, glute bridges

Optional but highly beneficial: Box step-downs (eccentric control for descending stairs) Single-leg balance work (technical terrain stability)

**Coaching rule:** Strength training stays consistent through all phases. Do not drop it when running volume increases. This athlete already does gym 4-5x per week — leverage this. Upper body gym sessions are fine — protect the leg sessions.

---

### HILL TRAINING — SOTA'S EMPHASIS

# 

B2M has constant undulation — no long climbs but hundreds of short ones. Stairs are the real killer. Walking hills is normal and efficient.

**A. Hill Repeats (once per week)**

- 6-10 × 60-90 seconds uphill
- Easy jog down
- Focus on form, not speed
- Can replace a tempo session during BUILD phase

**B. Stair Sessions (once per week minimum from BUILD)**

- 20-40 minutes continuous up/down
- Mix running + power hiking
- Use local coastal paths: Coogee Stairmaster, Bronte stairs, Bondi Icebergs stairs, Tamarama stairs
- Progress: climb only → climb + descent → repeat sets → loaded packs

**C. Long Runs on Hilly Terrain**

- Aim for 600-1,200m elevation in longest runs
- Prioritise rolling terrain over single big climbs
- Weekly elevation targets: BASE phase: 200-400m/week BUILD phase: 400-700m/week PEAK phase: 700-1,600m/week (target Sota's recommendation)

### SOTA'S PACING STRATEGY — MOST IMPORTANT MESSAGE

# 

"The first half must feel too easy."

**Why:**

- Course gets harder after 40km
- Heat builds through the day
- Stairs accumulate fatigue
- Nutrition becomes harder to manage
- Everyone blows up if they run first 30km like a road race

**Practical pacing rules:**

1. Run the first 20km at "ultra easy" — RPE 4-5
2. Walk every meaningful hill — even if you feel fresh
3. Walk all stairs early — save legs for later
4. Keep HR in Zone 2 throughout Legs 1-2
5. Eat early and often — don't wait for hunger
6. Don't chase other runners — the field spreads naturally

**The marathon split test:** Your feeling at the marathon mark (42km) predicts your finish.

- Feeling "good" at 42km → you're set for a strong back half
- Feeling "worked" at 42km → you're cooked for Legs 3-4 This athlete's primary fear is blowing up at km 50+. Conservative pacing through km 42 is the single biggest lever.

### COURSE SECTION TRAINING GUIDANCE (Sota Framework)

# 

**Km 0-20 (Bondi → Watsons Bay → The Gap):** Rolling coastal, stairs, crowds early Train: stair sessions, easy pacing discipline, technical footwork

**Km 20-40 (Harbour trails → Double Bay → Botanical Gardens):** Urban transitions, pavement, heat exposure Train: long steady efforts, heat acclimation, nutrition timing

**Km 40-55 (Harbour Bridge → Neutral Bay → Mosman):** More stairs, narrow paths, increasing fatigue Train: back-to-back long runs, power hiking, mid-run nutrition

**Km 55-70 (Spit Bridge → Manly Scenic Walkway):** Technical, steep short climbs, narrow sections Train: technical trail sessions, short hill repeats, obstacle pacing

**Km 70-80 (Manly → North Head → Finish):** Exposed, mentally tough, long runnable sections before finish Train: mental resilience, finishing strong on tired legs, MANDATORY North Head recce with fatigued legs

### WEEKLY TRAINING STRUCTURE (Sota's Framework)

# 

```
Weekly non-negotiables:
  1 long run (3-5 hours)
  1 back-to-back long run (2-3 hours)
  1 hill/stair session
  1 strength session minimum
  1 easy aerobic run
  Daily walking/standing (30-60 min)
```

**Key progressions:**

- Long run: increase by 15-20 minutes every 1-2 weeks
- Elevation: increase gradually, don't spike
- Back-to-back: increase volume every 2-3 weeks
- Strength: stays consistent — never drop it

**Race-specific blocks:**

```
8-10 weeks out:
  Highest volume
  Longest back-to-backs
  Most elevation
  This athlete: Weeks 15-17 (mid-Aug to early-Sep)

3-4 weeks out (peak long run):
  4-5 hour peak long run
  Peak elevation week
  This athlete: Week 20-21 (late Sep)

2 weeks out:
  Reduce volume
  Maintain intensity
  Keep hills but shorter
  This athlete: Weeks 22-23 (Oct 10-17)
```
